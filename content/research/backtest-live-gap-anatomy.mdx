We read the published research on why paper and live diverge, then checked it against 41 live sessions of our own. Most of what we saw agrees with the research. One standard measure misleads on quiet days.

## What does the published research say?

Researchers measure two different gaps.

Almgren and Chriss [2] set execution cost against the risk of waiting. That is the trade-off behind every order a live system sends.

Three limits stand out:

1. **Asset class.** Most of this work covers equities and factor portfolios.
2. **Frequency.** Results are annual or per trade. Order-level data is needed to split the implementation gap into delay, execution and missed orders.
3. **Benchmark.** We found no published benchmark for how closely a strategy's live daily P&L tracks its backtest.

So we measured our own.

## What did we test at MoNOR?

We tested a few strategies. Every strategy had a backtest of the same rules on the same days. We lined them up, day by day.

1. **Window.** 41 trading sessions between August and October 2026.
2. **Scope.** 14 strategies and 386 strategy-days with any activity.
3. **Basis.** Live P&L comes from actual fill prices. Both series exclude brokerage, taxes and exchange fees.
4. **Scale.** Each P&L is divided by that strategy's own typical day, so the charts carry no rupee values.
5. **Gap ratio.** Absolute gap divided by absolute backtest P&L.

**What the split shows:**

1. **Size dominates.** Same-sign size gaps are 51% of days and 59% of the gap.
2. **Sign flips are rare and costly.** They are 6% of days and 22% of the gap. 12 of the 22 fell on days when backtest P&L was under a quarter of a typical day. A small price difference can flip the sign of a near-zero day.
3. **Missed and extra positions are small.** Together 13 strategy-days and 7% of the gap.
4. **Live trails mostly on winning days.** On 207 same-sign winning days, live P&L was 5.6% of backtest P&L lower. On 144 same-sign losing days the shortfall was 0.6%.
5. **The tail is moderate.** The ten largest strategy-days hold 23% of the gap. The largest forty hold 49%.

## Can a daily percentage gap mislead?

Yes. The usual daily percentage gap divides by that day's backtest P&L. On a quiet day that number is close to zero.

## What happens when strategies are combined?

Gaps partly cancel. Pooled across strategies the gap ratio is 21%. At portfolio level it is **11%**.

## What did we learn?

1. **Match rate and gap ratio tell different stories.** Only 40% of strategy-days land within 10%, yet the portfolio gap is 11%.
2. **The denominator matters as much as the data.** The same session read 1,391% or 117% depending on the measure.
3. **Combining strategies absorbs gaps.** Portfolio level is about half the strategy-level figure.

## What can you check in your own data?

1. **Sort before averaging.** Split each strategy-day into the five types above. Size, sign and missed positions need different fixes.
2. **Use a stable denominator.** Divide the gap by average daily absolute backtest P&L.
3. **Report at two levels.** Compare strategy-level and portfolio-level gaps.

## What can this data not tell us?

1. **Cause.** Daily P&L cannot separate delay, execution cost and price-source differences. That needs fill-level data, which is our next step.
2. **Sample.** 41 sessions is one window, and some strategies have only a handful of active days.
3. **Direction.** The lower live P&L, mostly on winning days, is an observation. We have not tested a cause.

## Methodology note

**Source.** Live MoNOR daily data. Both backtest and live series are gross of explicit charges (brokerage, taxes and exchange fees). Every daily total reconciles to the source record. No rupee values, strategy names or thresholds appear in this article.

**Scale.** Each P&L is divided by the strategy's mean absolute backtest P&L over its active days.

**Types.** *Within 10%*: live is within 10% of backtest P&L. *Size gap*: same sign, outside 10%. *Sign flip*: both non-zero, opposite signs. *Backtest took it, live did not*: live is zero. *Live took it, backtest did not*: backtest is zero.

**Measures.** *Gap ratio*: sum of absolute gaps divided by sum of absolute backtest P&L. *Portfolio gap ratio*: the same measure on daily portfolio totals. *Capture*: slope of live on backtest through the origin, where both are non-zero. *Direction*: sum of live minus backtest, divided by sum of absolute backtest P&L, over same-sign strategy-days. *Naive gap*: absolute portfolio gap divided by that day's absolute backtest P&L. *Stable gap*: the same gap divided by the window's average daily absolute backtest P&L.

**Literature.** Citations were checked against publisher or repository records. Figures quoted from published studies are as reported by their authors.
